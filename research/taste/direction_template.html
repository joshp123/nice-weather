Where on earth is best all year round?

T-shirt weather

Cool hours count in the sun, hot hours in the shade, as you'd choose yourself. For scale: San Diego or Málaga at 1 pm in winter feels 22 °C in the sun; at 4 pm in July, Seville feels 35 °C in the shade and Austin 37 °C.

The walk home at 9 pm

San Diego at 9 pm feels about 12 °C in January and 25 °C in August. Below your setting, the day is Fair at best.

Humidity

Measured as dew point, which is what you feel as muggy; relative humidity changes with the temperature. Typical summer days: Seville 15 °C, San Diego and Málaga 18 °C, Austin 21 °C; Bangkok and Singapore 24 to 25 °C all year. Each degree past your setting costs a little more.

Rain

Rain between 10:00 and 9 pm. A third of your amount costs a fifth of the day; twice your amount costs most of it.

Wind

Wind already counts in how warm it feels. This is the nuisance on top: dust, sand, hair. Cape Town in January averages 25 km/h (the "Cape Doctor"); Rotterdam in January 17 km/h.

What a nice day is

T-shirt-and-shorts weather from 10:00 until the walk home at 9 pm. Each hour counts if it feels warm enough in the sun and not too hot in the shade. Cold costs faster than heat: an hour 5 °C below your lower setting no longer counts; above your upper setting it takes 10 °C. Rain, humidity and wind take a share off the day. If the walk home is colder than your evening setting, the day is Fair at best. A day is Nice when most of it works.

The same score grades each forecast day: Awful, Poor, Fair, Nice, Beautiful or Perfect.

You asked whether an average wind of 30 km/h should make a day Fair. Not on its own: wind already lowers how warm it feels, which is where most of its cost lies. At your setting, 30 km/h costs a tenth of the day and 45 km/h half.

I tested a sixth setting, a minimum of sunshine, and left it out. It barely changes anything, because days warm enough for a t-shirt are almost always sunny: nice days at every test place have a median of 8 to 10 sunny hours out of 12.

How the map keeps up with the sliders

Under 0.2 s for any change, anywhere on the map. Every 100 km square keeps a small sample of real days. When a slider moves, your device's graphics chip recounts them and adds the fine detail for height and coast pixel by pixel, the way Theo's map does. Nothing waits on a server while you drag. Sites does the heavy work in advance: it builds the samples, and the map at your default taste in full detail, every day, so the first view is instant.

I checked that the small day summary this needs gives the same answer as the full hour-by-hour calculation: the same verdict on 99 to 100% of days at 19 places, never more than 3 nice days a year apart. This page runs exactly that recount. The timing for the whole world on a phone is the first thing I measure when building.

What I'm fixing anyway

  • Coasts. Today coastal towns read 2 to 4 °C too cold, because each square mixes sea, coast and mountain. Taking temperature from the nearest land and correcting for height cuts the error to under 1 °C against weather stations. Cape Town still reads about 3 °C too hot in summer; that gets fixed before it goes live.
  • Rain. The data counts too many wet days in dry places: Málaga in January reads wet on 26% of days, its weather station 16.5%. I'll test Theo's correction against stations and keep whatever matches them.
  • Forecast and climate agree. Both use the same sun measure (from solar radiation) and the forecast adds gusts. 15 forecast days, as ECMWF gives; ECMWF's 9 km forecast when it becomes free.
  • The 20-year climate is built on Sites. It works; run one area at a time, the world would take weeks, so it runs in parallel.
