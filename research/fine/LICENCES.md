# Publication rights for fine temperature inputs

Checked 2026-10-17. This builds on round3/finedata/SOURCES.md and rechecks the primary notices. “Allow” below includes public redistribution of calculated temperature corrections, rather than merely displaying a source map. No accounts, credentials, email submissions or human contacts were used.

The strongest new route is **WorldClim 1.4 historical temperature under its specific CC BY-SA 4.0 grant**, with an ERA5 period adjustment, if validation supports it. It provides station-informed 30 arc-second detail without our having to fit potentially restricted foreign GHCN-Daily records. Publish the derived correction dataset under CC BY-SA 4.0, with attribution, source/version, changes and the licence link. Keep the application code's licence separate. CHELSA CC0 remains an unrestricted alternative, and PRISM/Daymet/SILO provide usable regional comparisons or anchors.

**GHCN-hourly is explicitly CC0 in its current NOAA dataset metadata and NOAA-managed AWS entry.** It offers a global station route if further fitting is necessary. The similarly named GHCN-Daily has conflicting primary notices: a new CC0 AWS entry but an old, still-linked product document prohibiting redistribution of foreign derived products. Do not silently treat the latter as public-domain station training data. This conflict does not prevent private validation; it does mean the most conservative Phase 2 build should avoid foreign GHCN-Daily fitting unless its selected records have independently verified open-provider rights.

## Other possible station/grid routes checked

- **TerraClimate:** the [current producer page](https://www.climatologylab.org/terraclimate.html) describes v1.1 as ERA5 anomalies superposed on WorldClim v1.4 and v2 climatologies. It does not state a data licence. Google's Earth Engine catalogue says CC0, but that is not a primary producer grant for the current downloadable v1.1 files, and its older description refers to v1.0. The producer says legacy files now require contact. This is insufficient to use TerraClimate as a licence workaround around WorldClim v2.1. **Not cleared for this build** unless a specific downloaded release's own licence establishes its rights. Direct WorldClim 1.4 BY-SA is clearer and finer.
- **Berkeley Earth:** [current producer page](https://berkeleyearth.org/data/) says data are generally CC BY-NC 4.0, with commercial permission by contact; 0.25° data also require a request. **Excluded** from an unrestricted, no-contact product input. Old claims that all Berkeley Earth data are open for all uses are stale.
- **Meteostat:** [licence page](https://dev.meteostat.net/license) explicitly gives redistributed Meteostat data CC BY 4.0, including commercial derivatives, but says it does not own meteorological data and distinguishes provider-direct Python downloads. Its declared licence is useful, yet it does not resolve a conflicting original-provider warning by itself. Prefer NOAA's directly and explicitly CC0 GHCNh grant for globally trained residuals. Do not mistake Meteostat software's MIT licence for data rights.
- **GHCN-M v4:** [current official directory](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ghcn/v4/) exposes TAVG release files rather than an equivalent ready TMAX/TMIN pair. No clearer temperature-extrema rights/acquisition advantage was established over GHCNh within this bounded research. **Not selected.**

## Scientific consequences of the legal choice

WorldClim 1.4's [methods](https://www.worldclim.org/data/v1.4/methods.html) say nominal 1960–1990 averages were used where possible, with some 1950–2000 and undated station means added. It is not an exact uniform baseline everywhere. A simple ERA5 anomaly can adjust the nominal climate period but cannot make every original station's averaging window known.

Its original interpolation used 14,835 TMIN/TMAX station locations. Unless its complete station list is excluded, comparing today's requested stations with WorldClim is **not spatially held-out validation of WorldClim itself**. We can truthfully hold every requested station out of our own fitting, and test a later observational period with a declared anomaly correction, but should not claim the original published grid never saw those stations. The same distinction applies to station-corrected regional grids.

For a new independently fitted global residual model, use GHCNh's explicit CC0 product, exclude requested held-out station IDs and co-located/renamed identities from training, preserve QC/source/coverage fields, and check hourly sampling against true extrema before converting to monthly anchors. U.S. federal GHCNd is suitable where true daily extrema improve those anchors. This is a viable route rather than a claim that global station correction is legally impossible.

The round3 CHELSA licence/access conclusions still hold. Its legally open fine grid does not by itself prove fog-coast accuracy: terrain downscaling cannot be assumed to reproduce a marine layer absent station correction. Select among these eligible routes by the parent's station experiment.

## Small anonymous ERA5 period-adjustment archive

[Planette’s producer repository](https://github.com/PlanetteAI/planette_era5_archive), current main verified 2026-10-17, explicitly grants **CC BY 4.0** for its derived ERA5 archive. Public derived normals/anomalies are allowed with ECMWF/C3S, NSF-NCAR and Planette citations, source/licence links and a description of modifications. The producer repository supplies the licence; this is not an inference from a third-party catalogue.

Anonymous access was actually verified: Icechunk `s3://planette-era5/ERA5_ic/month`, region `us-east-2`, group `single/0p25latx0p25lon`. The monthly time coordinate runs from April 1940 through September 2026, latitude 90 to −90, longitude −179.75 to 180. `t2m` is monthly mean temperature in kelvin. Its chunks are 30 months × 721 latitudes × 1440 longitudes, float32 with Zstd; each full global compressed chunk is about 93 MB. All chunk references are physical objects in the public bucket, not inaccessible upstream virtual files. A first real value read returned 288.285919 K (15.135919 °C), July 2020 at 37.75° N, −122.5° E.

The complete 1960–1990 plus 2016–2025 monthly-mean input needs approximately 1.5–1.8 GB of compressed chunks (2.04 GB decoded before chunk-edge overhead), making global mean-temperature anomalies practical on this Mac. Download once, cache on the SSD, calculate twelve monthly anomalies and discard scratch only by explicit policy. No old global hourly archive is necessary for the mean-anomaly variant.

**Do not treat `t2m_max`/`t2m_min` as monthly mean daily extrema.** The README calls them daily maxima/minima but also says it averages all hourly variables for daily files. Their stored attributes retain “since previous post-processing”, which may identify hourly forecast-interval extrema. The actual July 2020 San Francisco native-point probe returned `t2m_max` 15.284387 °C versus `t2m` 15.135919 °C: only 0.148468 °C higher. This is consistent with averaging hourly forecast-interval maxima and incompatible with treating it as a daily-high climatology for that location. The cached same-point Earthmover hourly series for July 2020 independently gives mean 15.135926 °C, mean UTC daily high 18.558630 °C and mean UTC daily low 12.696516 °C. Planette monthly mean agrees within 0.000007 °C, whereas its “max” is 3.274243 °C below the true mean daily high. Exclude its extrema variables as daily-extrema anchors; monthly mean `t2m` is numerically verified as the viable period-adjustment input. Evidence: `/tmp/planette_values.log` and `/tmp/planette_compare_cached.py`.

## Instrument extrema preserved in GHCN-hourly messages

The87 cached GHCNh Parquet schemas contain no structured temperature-maximum,
temperature-minimum or temperature-period columns. They do contain original
`REM` messages and their measurement, quality, report-type, source and source-
station fields. Actual SYNOP section3 extrema groups occur in47 cached files
across five stations. These are potentially useful CC0 instrument observations,
not merely maxima of sparse instantaneous samples.

The [NOAA AviationWeather guide](https://aviationweather.gov/help/data/) describes
METAR6-hour maximum/minimum and24-hour pair groups. SYNOP reporting periods
depend on regional rules; the
[NOAA-hosted WMO material](https://icoads.noaa.gov/etmc/VOS_Template_V8_FINAL.pdf)
does not provide a uniform global12/24-hour temperature field. GHCNh QC of
instantaneous temperature does not independently validate decoded REM extrema.
The [KNMI analysis](https://cdn.knmi.nl/system/data_center_publications/files/000/069/211/original/2011jd016687.pdf?1495621664=)
also shows that shorter reporting-window extrema can miss daily extrema.

A subsequent station fit may decode complete24-hour extrema, combine complete
shorter windows, or compare station/model extrema over identical windows. It
must preserve report period and QC. This establishes a viable licensed route,
not a tested drop-in global daily-extrema dataset or a fitted Phase1 product.
