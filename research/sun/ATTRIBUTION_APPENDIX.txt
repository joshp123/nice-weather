
SATELLITE SUNSHINE CORRECTION FOR NAMED PLACES

Sunshine and direct-sun warmth in the typical year are modified using independent
satellite radiation where the source-support table identifies coverage. The
accepted local high/low air-temperature corrections and uniform evening shift
are retained. The default-taste score samples and facts are derived together;
the overview map, changed tastes and ECMWF forecasts retain their existing data.
Sunshine is the mean daily share of daylight clock hours after 10:00 whose
hour-mean direct-normal irradiance reaches 300 W/m². It is not total cloud-free
sky fraction or the same metric as all-day ground-station sunshine duration.

The source-support table and manifest distinguish source products and periods.
Typical meteorological years select representative observed months; they are
not the complete hourly record or exact long-period sunshine-threshold climatology.
Two-year observed averages are likewise not long-term climate normals.

MODIFICATIONS AND LIMITS
Instantaneous radiation observations are linearly interpolated and integrated
at minute midpoints into local civil clock hours, using each source month’s
actual historical time-zone offsets. Monthly sunny-share offsets are fitted to
the exact eight representative weather days used by the shared place model.
Monthly/hourly radiation ratios adjust the direct-sun felt heating, preserving
zero native direct heating. No place-specific scoring exception is applied.
Air highs, 21:00 shade feels-like, dew point, rain and wind are not changed by
this correction. Radiation has coarser spatial detail than the 1 km temperature
anchors. Monthly bias correction cannot recover cloud/weather covariance on
individual days, and satellite retrievals have their own uncertainties.
