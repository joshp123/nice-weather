The product answers two questions: where will the next 15 days be like that, and where is it like that in a typical month, season or year, so you know where to spend the winter.

1. What a nice day is

A nice day is t-shirt-and-shorts weather for most of the day between 10:00 and 20:00: warm enough in the sun, not too hot in the shade, dry and not windy.

  • Warm enough means it feels at least 21 °C in the sun. Too hot means it feels over 30 °C in the shade. "Feels" includes humidity and wind, so humid heat fails and a cold wind counts. Sun counts when it is cool and shade when it is hot, as you would choose yourself.
  • Grades count the t-shirt hours out of ten: Perfect is all ten, Beautiful eight or nine, Nice six or seven, Fair three to five, Poor fewer. Awful is a day that never feels warmer than 12 °C, feels over 36 °C even in the shade, or has heavy rain.
  • 1 mm of rain or more makes a day Fair at best, 5 mm or more Poor at best. An average wind of 30 km/h or more makes it Fair at best. Cloud counts through warmth: a warm grey day can still be Nice; a cold sunny one cannot.

2. Cycling

Cycling uses the same t-shirt rule, but wind and rain count for more: an average wind of 20 km/h or more makes a day Fair at best, and any day with 1 mm of rain is Poor at best. A day is never better for cycling than for walking about.

Decide 2: keep Cycling like this? I recommend yes.

3. What a visitor can change

You said the product must be configurable, like Theo's. Your taste above is the default. Anyone can change:

  • the temperature that is warm enough for a t-shirt (21 °C in the sun by default);
  • the temperature that is too hot (30 °C in the shade);
  • how many hours of the day must be t-shirt weather (six);
  • Nice or Cycling.

The map then redraws for their taste. It is computed on Sites, the free server compute, so the phone does no heavy work. Expect a few seconds; I'll measure it before building. Theo's full builder (any condition, any range) is left out, because it turns the product into a form.
