What a nice day is

You can wear a t-shirt and shorts from 10:00 until you walk home at 9 pm: warm enough in the sun when it's cool, not too hot in the shade when it's hot, without rain, sticky air or strong wind.

  • Warm enough: it feels at least 21 °C in the sun. Cold costs a lot: each degree below counts heavily, and at 16 °C an hour no longer counts.
  • Too hot: it feels over 30 °C in the shade. Heat costs less: it takes 10 degrees more before an hour stops counting.
  • Sticky air: above a dew point of 18 °C it starts to cost; at 24 °C (Singapore) it is miserable.
  • Rain: 1 mm costs a little, 3 mm half the day, 10 mm all of it. Wind: it already makes it feel colder, so only a strong wind (25 km/h or more on average) costs extra.
  • Every hour from 10:00 to 9 pm is scored on how comfortable it is; the day's grade is the average. Nice or better means the day mostly works.

Why SoCal isn't nice all year

San Diego in January: the shade high is 20 °C, it feels warm enough for a t-shirt for about 4 of the 12 hours (midday), and by 9 pm it feels 11 to 12 °C. In April it is 6 hours and 15 °C at 9 pm. From June to October it is t-shirt weather nearly all day and 20 to 23 °C at 9 pm. Rain falls on about 1 day in 10 in January. So SoCal's winter is sunny, mild middays with cool evenings, not shorts all day.

San Francisco scores lower than SoCal, as it should, but our data still runs it about 4 °C too warm in summer, because a 28 km grid cell can't see the fog belt. Fixing coastal microclimates like that is my job, not a decision for you.

What a visitor can change

Your taste is the default. Five settings, each a plain choice about one thing:

  • How warm: mild, warm (yours), hot. Moves "warm enough" and "too hot" together by about 2 °C a step.
  • Evenings: a light layer is fine, or t-shirt until 9 pm.
  • Sticky air: don't mind, normal (yours), hate it.
  • Rain: showers are fine, normal (yours), dry only.
  • Wind: don't mind, normal (yours), calm only.

Cycling stops being separate scoring: it is the preset "calm only, dry only". It comes later, after the main product.

Speed. That is 162 possible tastes. Sites prepares the map for every one of them in advance, and your device fetches the neighbouring steps in the background, so changing a setting swaps one picture already in memory: well under 0.2 s, anywhere on the map. Forecast maps for unusual tastes are made the first time anyone asks and kept.

Decide: these five settings? I recommend yes. Nothing else is planned, to keep it simple.
